# Action Items

Action Items represent concrete follow-through that RAP believes the tenant team is expected to perform.

## What They Accomplish

Action Items help RAP turn communication and record context into operational accountability.

- They capture commitments made to customers or other external participants.
- They capture requested follow-ups, promised materials, and other clearly expected work.
- They help users see what still needs to happen, not just what was discussed.

In product terms, Action Items are one of the main ways RAP bridges conversation history and execution.

## How They Differ From Other RAP Outputs

Action Items are related to other RAP artifact types, but they are not interchangeable with them.

| Artifact | Main meaning |
|---|---|
| **Action Item** | A concrete task or follow-through expectation. |
| **Next Step** | The most meaningful move that should advance the record. |
| **Required Action** | A signal that a risk, blocker, or urgent issue needs attention. |

An Action Item is usually narrower and more operational than a Next Step. It is also more concrete than a Required Action, which is about urgency rather than task ownership.

## What Action Items Usually Include

Action Items commonly carry details such as:

- a clear task description
- an owner or responsible person
- a due date when one is known
- a result or completion condition
- related business context, such as the account, record, meeting, or communication they came from

The exact shape can vary by tenant and product flow, but the core idea is consistent: an Action Item should stand on its own as a meaningful piece of work.

## Lifecycle

Action Items are not static.

They can be:

- **opened** when a new follow-through expectation appears
- **updated** when scope, timing, or context changes
- **resolved** when the work is clearly complete
- **cancelled** when the work no longer makes sense
- **replaced** when a newer, better-framed item supersedes the earlier one

This matters because RAP is tracking business follow-through over time, not just extracting one task list from one message.

## What The Agent Should Know

The most useful assumptions are:

- Action Items should be concrete enough to act on.
- They are usually tied to something the tenant team is expected to do.
- They should not be inflated from vague interest, casual conversation, or broad strategic ideas.
- They can outlive the source communication that created them and evolve as the relationship evolves.
