# Meeting Artifacts

Meeting artifacts help RAP support the full meeting lifecycle: before the conversation, during the conversation, and after the conversation.

## Meeting Summary

Meeting Summary explains what happened in a meeting and why it mattered.

- It is created after the meeting has occurred and RAP has captured the meeting transcript.
- It is meant to help users quickly understand the outcome without reading the full transcript.
- It captures decisions, developments, concerns, and meaningful movement in the conversation.

If the underlying meeting data is weak, incomplete, or missing, the summary may also be limited.

## Meeting Prep

Meeting Prep is a structured brief for an upcoming conversation.

- It brings together what matters before the meeting.
- It highlights likely goals, open issues, recent context, and important people.
- It gives users a product-level view of the current state of the relationship before they join the call.

Meeting Prep can include ideas such as:

- the overall goal of the meeting
- the most important discussion objectives
- recent insights and risks
- current engagement context
- relevant history
- likely next steps
- short profiles of external attendees

The purpose of Meeting Prep is orientation. It helps users enter the conversation with the right context, not just a list of background facts.

## Meeting Eval

Meeting Eval assesses how well a meeting supported the intended business process.

- It looks at meeting quality, not just meeting content.
- It can reflect whether the conversation advanced discovery, qualification, negotiation, or another customer process.
- It is usually framed through a tenant-configured methodology or eval style.

Different tenants may use different frameworks, such as general selling quality, BANT, MEDDPICC, SPICED, Sandler, or other methodology-specific lenses. The important product idea is that eval reflects the tenant's preferred way of judging meeting effectiveness.

## Meeting Classification

Meeting Classification helps RAP organize meetings beyond one summary document.

It can include:

- **Conversation type** for the main category of the meeting
- **Tags** for additional themes discussed
- **Keywords** for notable tenant-relevant terms that explicitly appeared

Classification makes meetings easier to understand at a glance and easier to group into broader patterns across time.

## What The Agent Should Know

Meeting artifacts represent different kinds of meeting understanding:

- Summary explains what happened.
- Prep explains what matters before the meeting.
- Eval explains how well the meeting served the business process.
- Classification explains how the meeting should be organized and understood at a glance.

These artifacts are complementary. They should not be treated as interchangeable versions of the same thing.
