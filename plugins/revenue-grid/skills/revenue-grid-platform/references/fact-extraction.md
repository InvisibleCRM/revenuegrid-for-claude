# Fact Extraction

Fact extraction is how RAP turns raw source material into durable business memory.

## What It Accomplishes

Facts let RAP keep track of what has actually been learned over time.

- They capture important business information from CRM activity, communications, and other related inputs.
- They make useful context reusable across the product instead of trapping it inside one email, transcript, or note.
- They provide the foundation for summaries, predictions, meeting outputs, and other RAP artifacts.

Facts are one of the main ways RAP moves from source data to business understanding.

## What A Fact Is

A fact is a short, self-contained business statement.

A good fact is usually:

- **Specific** enough to be useful on its own
- **Neutral** in tone rather than opinionated or promotional
- **Granular** enough to express one meaningful point at a time
- **Attributed** clearly enough that the source of the information is understandable
- **Grounded** in one business context instead of mixing unrelated contexts together

Facts are meant to be readable pieces of business memory, not fragments that require the original source to make sense.

## What Facts Are Not

Facts are not meant to be:

- full transcript replacements
- generic conversation summaries
- copies of signatures, metadata, or system noise
- speculative statements with no clear business value
- containers for sensitive personal information

## Main Sources

Facts can come from several kinds of product context, including:

- emails and attachments
- meetings and transcripts
- CRM updates
- calendar changes
- user notes
- user feedback on RAP outputs

The source matters because it affects both the point of view and the confidence RAP places on the information.

## Internal And External Perspective

Facts also carry a point of view.

- **External facts** reflect what customers or other outside participants said, did, or signaled.
- **Internal facts** reflect the tenant team's actions, notes, interpretations, or record updates.

When internal and external information conflict, RAP generally treats the external point of view as the stronger indicator of customer reality.

## What The Agent Should Know

The most important practical assumptions are:

- Facts are selective, not exhaustive.
- Facts are meant to preserve useful business meaning rather than source formatting.
- Facts can accumulate over time to form a longer-term picture of the relationship or record.
- Facts are often a better unit of reasoning than a raw communication because they are already distilled into business-relevant context.

For the agent, facts are best understood as RAP's working memory: compact, structured, and designed to support reasoning across time.
